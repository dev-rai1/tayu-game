# Student Demo Feedback — July 30, 2026

This note records anonymized product feedback from a middle-school demo tester. Personal contact information is intentionally excluded.

## What worked

- The colorful visual style and overall layouts were engaging.
- The Lemonade Stand visuals and profit-making activity were the strongest part of the demo.
- The Budget Town savings graph made progress easy to see.
- The Bank of TAYU and Money Garden felt lively because characters and locations were visible.
- The app generally functioned well and the tester was interested in trying a future version.

## Problems observed

- The Market did not make the first action obvious enough.
- Lemonade Stand controls did not clearly explain what each button would do.
- Budget Town instructed the learner to follow arrows, but the arrows were difficult to find.
- Money Garden could leave the learner unsure how to continue.
- Guidance text could overlap other text.
- The avatar creator needed more clothing variety.

## Changes in this pull request

- Expand shirt, bottom, and shoe color choices in the avatar creator.
- Separate the nearby-action chip from the persistent coach so text cards do not stack.
- Add a tall gold destination beacon and stronger route markers.
- Rewrite accessible-mode actions as explicit, numbered steps, especially for the Market and Lemonade Stand.
- Add a clear “Do this now” instruction and continuation button in Money Garden.
- Improve live-region labels, focus indicators, wrapping, and scrolling for screen-reader and keyboard users.

## Future module concept: Smart Shopping Challenge

Create a store-based module where learners buy things they want while considering more than the sticker price.

### Learning goals

- Compare wants with available money and higher-priority needs.
- Distinguish low upfront price from long-term value.
- Consider durability, warranties, repair costs, and replacement frequency.
- Recognize that food and other perishable products can expire or go bad.
- Practice reading simple product details before purchasing.

### Possible gameplay loop

1. Give the learner a budget and a short wish list.
2. Offer several versions of each product with different prices, quality, durability, and shelf life.
3. Let time pass after the purchase.
4. Reveal whether an item breaks, needs repair, or expires.
5. Show total cost over time and let the learner revise the decision.
6. Reward choices that balance enjoyment, reliability, and financial responsibility rather than simply choosing the cheapest item.

This concept belongs in the future-module backlog; it is documented here rather than added to the current five-module release without curriculum and playtesting review.
